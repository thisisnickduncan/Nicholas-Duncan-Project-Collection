## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment.** The page could not be lifted onto another product without breaking. The measurement typography — IBM Plex Mono, tabular figures, slashed zero, reserved strictly for real numbers — is doing genuine semantic work: seeing `1.2% → 14.1%` set in mono tells you it came off an instrument, and seeing prose set in Plex Sans tells you it did not. The case study's margin-annotation layout (PROBLEM / APPROACH / OUTCOME held in the left margin, sticking while the passage scrolls) is specific to a body of work that argues rather than displays.

The weakness is that the signature is applied indiscriminately. When "4 strategic frameworks applied" gets the same instrument readout as "1.2% → 14.1%", the device stops meaning *measured outcome* and starts meaning *number we had*. Two of the seven projects have real measurements; the rest are being dressed to match.

**Deterministic scan.** `detect.mjs --json app components` returned `[]`, exit 0. Zero findings across 32 component files. Contrast sampling across 11 distinct text styles on the case study found zero pairs below 4.5:1 against the paper ground. No horizontal overflow at 390px on the work index. All three icon-only buttons carry `aria-label`.

The detector confirms the foundation is clean. Every issue below is compositional, which is exactly the class of problem a deterministic scan cannot see.

**Visual overlays.** Not attempted; the findings came from full-page capture and direct layout measurement instead.

## Overall Impression

The type system, palette, and case study layout are working. The failure is concentrated in one decision, repeated: **a fixed landscape frame wrapped around content that is not landscape and not big enough to fill it.** Fix the frames and the site stops looking like it is waiting for assets that never arrived.

## What's Working

**The case study page.** The margin-held section labels, the outcome strip at the foot, and the prose rhythm make a long technical argument genuinely readable. This page carries the site.

**Metric provenance.** Every number in the hero, the share card, and the work rows is read out of `data/projects.ts` by slug. The hero and the case studies are structurally incapable of contradicting each other.

**The restraint in motion.** Page transitions are a 180ms crossfade, reveals are 340ms with no blur, the progress bar has no spring. Nothing animates to prove it can.

## Priority Issues

**[P1] The case study hero fills 20% of its frame.** Measured: a 260×589 image centred in a 1104×687 panel. `ScreenshotPanel` caps the image at `max-h-[70vh]` but lets the panel run the full column width, so a portrait phone capture floats in a wide landscape box with 420px of empty surface on either side. The largest element on the page is four fifths air.
*Why it matters:* This is the first evidence a visitor sees that the thing was actually built. Presented in an empty frame it reads as a broken image rather than a product.
*Fix:* Let the panel width follow the image's aspect ratio instead of the column's. For a portrait capture, cap the frame near the image's rendered width and centre the frame, not the image inside an oversized frame.
*Suggested command:* `/impeccable layout`

**[P1] The work row visual is an empty box three times out of four.** Same root cause, different component. `RowVisual` forces `aspect-[16/10]` on every project. The Telegram digest — the only real screenshot — renders 99px wide inside a 425px panel. The other rows put one or two small numbers in a box of the same size, and on mobile those boxes are near-empty rectangles roughly 200px tall.
*Why it matters:* This is the first screen of the work index and the middle of the homepage. Four rows of mostly-empty panels is the single strongest signal that the site is a template with the content not yet filled in — the exact complaint that started this work.
*Fix:* Stop treating the visual slot as one shape. A project with a screenshot gets a frame sized to that screenshot. A project with real outcome numbers gets a typographic block that fills its space, not two lines floating in a box. A project with neither gets no panel and lets the text run wider.
*Suggested command:* `/impeccable layout`

**[P1] Case study prose runs 83 characters per line.** `max-w-[68ch]` sets 734px, but `ch` measures the width of "0", which in IBM Plex Sans is materially wider than the average lowercase letter — so 68`ch` buys about 83 real characters. The comfortable range is 45–75.
*Why it matters:* Read is the mode that matters most here. The case studies are the argument; long measures make readers lose the line return and skim instead.
*Fix:* Reduce to roughly `max-w-[56ch]` to land near 66 real characters, and verify by counting rendered characters rather than trusting the unit.
*Suggested command:* `/impeccable typeset`

**[P2] The resume embed is the tallest block on the homepage and sits above the work.** A `clamp(30rem,60vh,44rem)` PDF viewer sits between the about statement and Selected work.
*Why it matters:* A visitor scrolling for evidence of what he has built passes a full-height embedded document viewer first. The resume is the least differentiated thing on the site and currently gets the most vertical space on it.
*Fix:* Either move the resume below Selected work, or reduce it to a document card on every breakpoint rather than only on mobile.
*Suggested command:* `/impeccable layout`

**[P2] The outcome metric strip goes ragged when a value wraps.** On the Telegram case study, "On the hour, to the second" wraps to two lines while its two neighbours stay on one, so the three labels underneath land on different baselines.
*Why it matters:* The metric strip is the signature device. Ragged is the one thing an instrument readout cannot be.
*Fix:* Give the value a fixed line allowance so labels align regardless of wrap, or set the value in a size that keeps this string on one line.
*Suggested command:* `/impeccable polish`

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (first-timer).** Lands on the homepage, reads a strong claim, then scrolls into a black rectangle where the resume is meant to be — the PDF renders in real Chrome, but in any browser without a PDF plugin, or with one blocked, Jordan sees a void. Then four project rows whose picture-boxes are empty. Nothing on the page tells Jordan the boxes are meant to hold anything, so the most likely reading is "this site is broken", not "this is minimal".

**Casey (distracted, one-handed, on a phone).** The work index puts a near-empty ~200px panel after every project, roughly doubling the scroll distance to reach the seventh project. Casey is paying for four screens of empty rectangles. Filters live at the top and never return, so after scrolling to project six there is no way to re-filter without scrolling back.

**Riley (stress tester).** Reloads the homepage and screenshots it before scrolling: the digest image is `loading="lazy"` and simply is not there. Sets the browser to reduced motion: reveals resolve correctly, no content is stranded mid-transition — this holds up. Opens a case study with no screenshot, no paper, and no demo: the header ends after the tools list with no visual at all, so different case studies have visibly different anatomies.

## Minor Observations

- "Contact Me" is the only title-case heading on the site; "Selected work" and "Resume" are sentence case. It is your copy, so I have not touched it, but the inconsistency is visible.
- Both theme-toggle buttons (desktop and mobile) exist in the DOM at once. They are display-hidden rather than duplicated in the accessibility tree, so this is not a defect — worth knowing it is deliberate.
- The row visual panels use `bg-surface` on `paper`, a very close value pair. The frames read as holes rather than as raised panels; a slightly stronger separation would make them look intentional even before they are filled.
- The SwingScore row shows "6M+ rows of data processed" — the identical metric already stated in the hero, within one scroll of it.

## Questions to Consider

- If two of seven projects have real measured outcomes, should the other five be presented in the same instrument language at all, or does the device get stronger by being rarer?
- The case study page is markedly better than the homepage. What would the homepage look like if it borrowed the case study's margin-annotation structure instead of its own section stack?
- What is the visual slot in a work row actually for — proof that the thing exists, or rhythm on the page? If it is proof, three of four rows currently have none.
